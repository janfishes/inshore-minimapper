Import a track or a file GPX off the plotter card, or a CSV
The ACTIVE LOG is the one worth exporting. On the ECHOMAP, saving a track throws away the timestamps and the depths and keeps only the shape — every usable sounding lives in ACTIVE LOG, and that is a rolling buffer, so export before it wraps.

Garmin writes metres even when the screen says feet. The gpxx schema defines depth in metres and the unit converts on the way out. This converts once, and says so on every import — if a number looks four times too shallow, that is the trap you are looking at.
Repeat measurements the same water twice, months apart

A single sounding says what the bottom was. The same spot measured again says which way it is going — and that is the one thing no survey of this water can tell you.

Nothing armed. Mark a spot or draw a line below, then come back to it.
A tape or a lead line is the better instrument for this, and it is not close. Your own boat data measures why: crossing the same 15 m of water twice in one afternoon — with nothing having moved — the sounder disagreed with itself by about 1 ft typically and over 4 ft one time in ten, because a boat crossing a slope 10 m to one side is reading a different piece of bottom. A tape at a marked spot has none of that: no transducer offset, no cone spreading with depth, no drift, and you can stand on the same foot of bottom every time. So a mark measured by hand can show a change of half a foot honestly, where a line run with the sounder cannot show three.

Both are worth having — a line finds where the bottom is moving, a mark measures how much. Every reading keeps the method it was taken with, and the history below is judged against that method's own noise, never one blanket number.
Spots what each one really holds at dead low
A spread over 3 ft is not one depth — it is a slope, and no single number describes it. Either the pass crossed the edge of a hole or a bar, or a reading was taken off bottom.

Soundings are kept on this device and nowhere else. Take the CSV or the backup off it now and then — a cleared browser takes the lot.
